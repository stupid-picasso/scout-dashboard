CP, HP & IV, explained

one page · read in 4 min
CP
Combat Power

A single number summarizing overall strength. Goes up as you level up a Pokemon and with better IVs. Higher isn't always better — PvP leagues cap it.

HP
Hit Points

How much damage a Pokemon can take before fainting. Driven by the species' base Stamina, its level, and its Stamina IV.

IV
Individual Values

Hidden per-catch bonus, 0–15 each for Attack, Defense, Stamina. Randomized every catch — genetics, basically.

For Raids & PvE

IV barely matters. What matters: highest CP you can field, and the right moveset (type advantage beats stats). Anything 80%+ IV is fine to invest in.

Rule of thumb: build a roster of high-CP attackers per type, don't obsess over their exact IVs.

For PvP Battles

Each league caps CP: Great ≤1500, Ultra ≤2500, Master uncapped. Under a cap, low Attack IV is often good — it keeps CP down while Defense/HP stay high, so you hit the cap at a higher level with more bulk.

This is why your export has a separate Rank % per league — not just one IV number. Trust that column over raw IV% for PvP picks.

Leveling up

Costs Stardust + Candy per half-level. Level cap is 40 normally, 50/51 with rare XL Candy from very high-level catches or Best Buddy rewards.

Lucky Pokemon

Earned by trading. Cost half Stardust to power up, and every stat IV is guaranteed 12+ — a built-in floor on quality.

Shadow / Purified

Shadow: +20% Attack, -20% Defense — strong for raids, riskier in PvP. Purifying removes the boost/penalty and raises IVs slightly.

BEGINNER CHECKLIST
1. Don't power up everything — wait until you know a Pokemon's role.
2. For raids: chase high CP + correct type moveset.
3. For PvP: check the league Rank % column, not raw IV%.
4. Trade duplicates for a shot at Lucky (cheaper, better floor).
5. Low IV + no PvP rank + no raid role → safe to transfer.
6. Re-scan after big updates; rankings shift with new Pokemon.
